What I build

Four kinds of problems
I solve most often.

Web applications

Custom tools built around how you actually work

Internal dashboards, client portals, booking and inventory systems. Built for your process instead of bending your process around software that almost fits.

Integration

Getting your systems to talk to each other

Payment gateways, third-party services, your accounting software, a mobile app that needs a backend. Clean APIs, documented, and stable enough to forget about.

Artificial intelligence

AI that does a job, not a demo

Reading and extracting data from documents, searching your own company's information in plain language, sorting and classifying at volume. The useful kind — with cost controls and sensible fallbacks when the model gets it wrong.

Automation

The work nobody should be doing by hand

Manual data entry, weekly reports rebuilt from scratch, files moved between systems one at a time. If someone on your team repeats it every week, it can usually be automated in days.

How it works

From first call to
handover.

STEP 01

Talk

A free call. What's the problem, who uses it, and what does finished look like. No obligation, and I'll say so if I'm not the right fit.

STEP 02

Scope

A written proposal: what's included, what isn't, a timeline, and a fixed price. You approve it before any code is written.

STEP 03

Build

Regular check-ins with something you can actually click and test — not a status report describing progress you can't see.

STEP 04

Launch

Deployed, documented, and handed over with the accounts in your name. A support window is included after launch.

About

Based in Malaysia,
working anywhere.

I'm a software developer who works across the full stack, with most of my depth on the backend — the services, data, and infrastructure that decide whether a product is reliable or merely finished.

A lot of my recent work sits where backend engineering meets AI: systems that read documents, find the right answer inside a pile of company data, and handle the repetitive work people shouldn't be doing manually.

What I enjoy most is the messy early part of a project — when a client knows something about their process is broken but isn't yet sure what to build. Turning that into a clear scope, and then into working software, is most of the job.

Questions

Before you get in touch.

What does a project cost?

It depends on scope, so I quote a fixed price after we've talked — you'll know the number before you commit to anything. As a rough guide, a focused internal tool starts around [RANGE], and a full application with users and accounts starts around [RANGE].

How long will it take?

A single-purpose tool or automation is usually 2–4 weeks. A full application with user accounts, an admin side, and deployment is typically 6–12 weeks. I'll give you a dated timeline in the proposal.

Can you work on something that already exists?

Yes. Taking over an existing codebase, extending it, or fixing what a previous developer left behind is common work. I'll review it first and tell you honestly whether repairing or rebuilding is the better spend.

What happens after launch?

A support window is included, covering bugs and small adjustments. After that, ongoing maintenance is available monthly, or you can hand the project to your own team — it's documented for exactly that.

Do I own the code?

Yes. The code, the accounts, and the server are in your name. You're free to take the project elsewhere at any point.
